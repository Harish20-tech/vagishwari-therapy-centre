What is occupational therapy?

Occupational therapy (OT) helps children do the everyday "occupations" of childhood: playing, learning, writing, dressing, eating and getting along with others. When a child struggles with these, it is often because of difficulties with fine motor control, body coordination, sensory processing or attention.

Our sessions are structured as play, so children enjoy coming. Every child starts with an assessment, and parents receive simple activities to continue at home.

Occupational therapy services we offer

Sensory integration therapy

For children who are over- or under-sensitive to sound, touch, movement or textures, helping them feel calmer and more regulated.

Fine motor skills

Hand strength, grip, cutting, buttoning and using small objects with control.

Handwriting readiness

Pencil grasp, letter formation, copying and writing speed for school.

Gross motor & balance

Coordination, balance, posture and body awareness for play and sport.

Daily living skills

Dressing, eating, toileting and routines that build independence.

Attention & sitting tolerance

Helping children sit, focus and complete tasks at home and in class.

Signs your child may benefit from OT

  • Difficulty holding a pencil or crayon
  • Messy or slow handwriting
  • Clumsiness or frequent falls
  • Very sensitive to noise, touch or clothes
  • Constantly seeking movement or crashing
  • Trouble sitting still or focusing
  • Picky eating linked to textures
  • Difficulty dressing or with daily routines

Occupational therapy often helps children with autism, ADHD, developmental delay, learning difficulties and sensory processing difficulties. It can be combined with speech therapy and special education at the same centre.

Frequently asked questions

What is occupational therapy (OT) for children?

Occupational therapy helps children build the skills they need for everyday life: using their hands, managing their senses, paying attention, dressing, eating and getting ready for school. Sessions are play-based so children enjoy them.

How do I know if my child needs occupational therapy?

Common signs include difficulty holding a pencil or crayon, poor handwriting, clumsiness, being very sensitive to sounds, touch or textures, seeking a lot of movement, trouble sitting still or focusing, and difficulty with dressing or eating. An assessment will tell you whether therapy will help.

Do you offer occupational therapy for children with autism or ADHD?

Yes. Many children with autism, ADHD or developmental delays benefit from occupational therapy for sensory processing, attention and daily living skills. Therapy can be combined with speech therapy and special education at the same centre.
