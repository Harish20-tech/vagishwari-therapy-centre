What is stammering?

Stammering, also called stuttering, is when the flow of speech is interrupted by repeated sounds or words, stretched sounds, or blocks where no sound comes out. It is common in young children and can continue into adulthood.

Therapy teaches techniques for smoother speech and builds confidence in speaking situations. For young children we work closely with parents; for older children and adults we also work on the worry and avoidance that stammering can cause.

Signs of stammering

  • Repeating sounds or words (b-b-ball)
  • Stretching sounds (sssssun)
  • Getting stuck with no sound coming out
  • Tension in the face or body while speaking
  • Avoiding certain words or speaking situations
  • Stammering that has lasted more than six months

Frequently asked questions

Can stammering be cured?

Many young children stop stammering with early therapy. Older children and adults can learn techniques that make speech much smoother and easier to manage.

At what age should a child who stammers see a therapist?

If stammering has lasted more than six months, is getting worse, or is worrying your child, an assessment is recommended.

Do you treat adults who stammer?

Yes. We see teenagers and adults and focus on practical speaking situations such as interviews, presentations and phone calls.
