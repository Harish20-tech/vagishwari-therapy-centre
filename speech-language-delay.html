What is speech and language delay?

A child has a speech and language delay when they understand or use fewer words than expected for their age. By age 2, most children use about 50 words and put two words together.

Delays can have many causes, including hearing difficulties, autism or simply needing extra support. Our assessment checks understanding, speech, play and hearing, and therapy then builds your child's words and sentences step by step.

Signs your child may have a speech delay

  • No babbling by 12 months
  • No single words by 18 months
  • Fewer than 50 words or no two-word phrases by age 2
  • Not following simple instructions
  • Using gestures or pulling you instead of words
  • Speech that is much less clear than other children of the same age

Frequently asked questions

Should I wait for my child to start talking on their own?

Some children do catch up, but many need support. An early assessment does no harm and helps you know whether therapy is needed.

Can screen time cause speech delay?

Too much screen time can reduce the talking and play children need for language. We guide parents on balancing screens with language-rich play.

Will you check my child's hearing?

Yes. Hearing problems can cause speech delay, so we can test your child's hearing at our centre.
