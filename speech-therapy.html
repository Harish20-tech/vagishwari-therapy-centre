What is speech therapy?

Speech therapy helps with any difficulty in understanding language, speaking clearly, using words and sentences, voice, fluency or social communication. Therapy is planned by a qualified speech-language pathologist after a detailed assessment.

Sessions use play, pictures and real conversations, and parents or family members are shown simple activities to practise at home between sessions.

Speech therapy services we offer

Speech and language delay

For children who are late to talk or use fewer words than expected for their age.

Articulation and pronunciation

Clear production of sounds that a child or adult finds hard to say.

Stammering (stuttering)

Fluency therapy for smooth, confident speech at any age.

Voice therapy

For hoarse, strained or tired voice, including teachers and professional voice users.

Speech after stroke

Therapy for aphasia and dysarthria after stroke or brain injury.

Social communication

Conversation, turn-taking and play skills, including for children with autism.

Signs someone may benefit from speech therapy

  • Not saying single words by 18 months
  • Not joining two words by 2 years
  • Speech that family or strangers find hard to understand
  • Repeating or getting stuck on sounds and words
  • Hoarse or strained voice that lasts more than two weeks
  • Difficulty following instructions or answering questions
  • Speech or language changes after a stroke

Frequently asked questions

At what age can a child start speech therapy?

Speech therapy can start as early as a concern is noticed, even before age 2. Early support usually gives the best results.

How long does speech therapy take?

It depends on the difficulty and the child or adult. After the assessment we explain the plan, how often sessions are needed and how progress will be reviewed.

Do you offer speech therapy for adults?

Yes. We see adults for voice problems, stammering, pronunciation and speech and language difficulties after stroke.
