What is swallowing and feeding therapy?

Swallowing difficulty (dysphagia) can cause coughing or choking while eating, food sticking in the throat, or chest infections. Feeding difficulty in children can show as very picky eating, gagging or trouble moving from purees to solid food.

A speech-language pathologist assesses how the mouth and throat muscles work and then teaches exercises, safe swallowing strategies and the right food and drink textures.

Signs of a swallowing or feeding difficulty

  • Coughing or choking while eating or drinking
  • Food or drink coming back through the nose
  • Feeling of food sticking in the throat
  • Wet or gurgly voice after swallowing
  • Very long mealtimes or weight loss
  • A child who gags often or refuses most textures

Frequently asked questions

Who needs swallowing therapy?

Adults after stroke or with neurological conditions, older adults with swallowing changes, and children with feeding difficulties can all benefit.

Is swallowing therapy done by a speech therapist?

Yes. Speech-language pathologists are trained to assess and treat swallowing and feeding difficulties.

Can therapy be done at home?

Yes. Home visits are available, which is helpful for adults recovering from stroke.
